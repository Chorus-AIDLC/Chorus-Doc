By default everyone in your workspace can see and work on every project. When some work
should stay with a smaller team, make the project, or the whole project group, **private**
and choose who gets in and at which level. This guide covers both levels and how they
combine.

## Public and private

Every project and every project group is either **Public** or **Private**.

- **Public** — everyone in the workspace (and the agents they own) can see it. On a public
  project everyone can also create and edit its ideas, proposals, documents, and tasks.
- **Private** — only its members (and the agents those members own) can see it. For anyone
  else it does not exist: it is left out of the project list, the sidebar, search, and
  dashboards, and opening a link to it shows a "page could not be found" message.

New projects and groups are public unless you choose otherwise (a new project inside a
private group is always private). Projects created before private access was available stay
public.

On the Projects page and in the project overview, a private project carries a **Private
project** badge. A private group shows a lock next to its name (hover it for an
explanation), and the sidebar shows the same lock next to private projects.

## Roles

Members of a private project or group have one of three roles:

| Role | What it allows |
| --- | --- |
| **Viewer** | Read everything in the project, including comments. Cannot create or edit anything, post comments, answer elaboration questions, or claim work. |
| **Editor** | Everything a Viewer can do, plus create and edit ideas, proposals, documents, and tasks, comment, claim work, approve proposals, and verify tasks. |
| **Admin** | Everything an Editor can do, plus change visibility, manage members, and (on a private project) edit its settings, move it, or delete it. |

On a **public** project there are no Viewers: everyone works as at least an Editor, and its
general settings, group moves, and deletion stay open to everyone. Only an Admin can make it
private or manage its members.

Whoever creates a project becomes its Admin. For projects created before private access was
available, the first user who joined the workspace is the Admin.

A Viewer sees a banner at the top of the project: **You have view-only access to this
project. Ask a project admin for Editor access to make changes.** Buttons that would change
something, such as **New Idea**, are hidden or disabled, and tasks cannot be dragged on the
board.

## Make a project private

When you create a project, the **New Project** dialog has a **Visibility** choice:
**Public** (everyone in the workspace can view and edit this project) or **Private** (only
listed members and their agents can access this project). Whichever you pick, you become
its Admin. See [Create a project](/guides/create-a-project/).

To change an existing project:

1. Open the project and select **Settings** in the top-right of its overview. The **Project
   settings** dialog opens; select the **Access** tab.
2. Under **Visibility**, choose **Public** or **Private**.
3. Chorus asks you to confirm and shows an **Access impact** summary of how many people gain
   or lose access. Select **Change visibility**. Making a project private removes access for
   everyone who is not a member, right away; making it public opens it to the whole
   workspace.

A project inside a private group cannot be made public.

Members are kept when a private project becomes public, so you can switch it back later
without rebuilding the list.

## Manage members

Only Admins can change members. Others see the list with the note **Only project admins can
change visibility or manage members.** In **Project settings → Access**:

- **Add member** — select **Select a user**, search by name or email, pick a role (Editor by
  default), and select **Add**.
- **Change a role** — pick a different role in the member's row.
- **Remove** — select the trash button in the member's row.

A project always keeps at least one Admin, so Chorus refuses to remove or demote the last
one. Every change takes effect immediately: a removed member stops receiving the project's
live updates at once, the project is gone from their lists the next time they load them, and
opening it shows a "page could not be found" message.

### Agents follow their owner

Agents are never members themselves. An agent can see and do in a project exactly what its
owner can, and its own permissions still apply on top. An agent with no owner can only work
in public projects. To give an agent access to a private project, add its owner.

### Mentions and assignment

In a private project you can only @mention, and assign work to, people who can see it (and
their agents). Assignees need at least Editor. Someone outside the project is never notified
about it, even if they created or were assigned something there before it became private.

## Private project groups

A project group can be private too, so you can manage access for many projects in one
place.

- **Group roles apply live to every project in the group.** A group Editor is an Editor in
  each of its private projects; when you change or remove a group member, every project
  follows immediately.
- **Projects can add access, never take it away.** You can grant someone a higher role on a
  single project, or let in someone from outside the group. Their effective role is the
  higher of their group role and their project role. A project cannot lower or remove a role
  that comes from the group.
- **Group Admins are Admins of every project in the group**, whether the group is public or
  private.
- **Private groups contain only private projects.** A project created inside a private group
  is private and inherits the group's roles; its creator also gets an Admin role on that
  project.

In a project's **Access** tab, each member shows where their role comes from: **Inherited
from group**, **Direct project grant**, or **Direct + inherited**, and the role that applies
(**Effective**). A member who only has an inherited role cannot be changed or removed there,
and a direct role cannot be set below the inherited one; change inherited roles in the group.

To manage a group, select **View Dashboard** on its card on the Projects page, then **Manage
Group**. For a group Admin the dialog has the group's name and description, its
**Visibility**, its **Members** (added and changed the same way as on a project), and
**Delete this group**. In a public group anyone can open **Manage Group** and edit the name
and description, but only group Admins can change visibility or members. In a private group,
other group members see **View members** instead, a read-only list of members. A group
without an explicit Admin is administered by the first user who joined the workspace, shown
as **Automatic admin**; adding an explicit Admin replaces that.

### Someone with access to only one project

You can give a person a role on one project in a private group without making them a group
member. They can see the group's name and description, and only the projects they have
access to. Counts, dashboards, and search show nothing else from the group, and they cannot
see the group's members or create projects in it.

### Make a group private or public

- **Public → Private**: every public project in the group becomes private and inherits the
  group's roles. Existing project members are kept.
- **Private → Public**: the group becomes discoverable by everyone, but its projects keep
  their current visibility and members. Nothing private is published.

Before either change, Chorus shows an **Access impact** summary: how many people gain or
lose access, how many get higher or lower permissions, and how many projects are affected.
Select **Change visibility** only if that matches what you intend. If access changed before
you confirm, Chorus shows a refreshed summary for you to review and confirm again.

## Move a project between groups

To move a project, drag it on the Projects page into another group, or into
**Ungrouped**. Chorus asks **Move project to another group?** and, because group roles follow
the group, shows the same **Access impact** summary. Select **Confirm Move** to go ahead.

Moving a private project, or moving any project into or out of a private group, needs you
to be an Admin of the group it leaves (or of the project, if it is ungrouped) and of the
group it joins. A public project moved into a private group becomes private.

A project takes its group roles from the group it is in. When you move it into another
group, the old group's roles stop applying and the new group's roles apply instead. When you
move a project to **Ungrouped**, everyone keeps the access they had at that moment: their
group roles are copied onto the project, and a private project stays private with at least
one Admin. Nothing becomes public by accident. The same happens when you delete a group and
choose **Move N projects to Ungrouped**; the other option, **Delete N projects
permanently**, deletes them.
