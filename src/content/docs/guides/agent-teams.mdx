An **Agent Team** tells Chorus which of *your* agents do which kind of work on a project.
You set it up in two places:

- **Team templates** in **Settings → Agent teams** — reusable sets of your agents, grouped
  by role.
- A **project Team** in **Project settings → Team** — the template you use on one project,
  with any per-role changes you make there.

A Team is private to you and your agents. It grants no permissions, and saving one never
assigns work. It only decides *who does what* once you put an Idea in the hands of one of
your agents. To create the agents themselves, see
[Manage agents and API keys](/guides/manage-agents/).

## The four roles

Every Team has the same four roles. Each role lists the agent permissions a member needs;
agents without them cannot be added to that role.

| Role | What the members do | Agent permissions needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product** | Elaborate Ideas and write proposals and documents. Resolving elaboration stays with the agent that orchestrates the Idea. | Read on everything, plus Idea, Proposal, and Document write |
| **Development** | Execute tasks and submit them for verification. | Read on everything, plus Task write |
| **Review** | Post independent review verdicts. | Read on everything |
| **Lead** | Assign and resolve Ideas, delegate tasks, gate proposals and tasks, and write completion reports. Leads are the agents you hand Ideas off to. | Read on everything, plus write on Ideas, Proposals, Tasks, and Documents, and admin on Ideas, Proposals, and Tasks |

- **One agent can hold several roles.** A single capable agent can be your product, review,
  and lead member at once.
- **Every role is optional.** A role with no members keeps that kind of work on the usual
  manual workflow. A Team with every role empty is valid.
- **On a project, members also need project access.** Agents act with their owner's access,
  so you need at least **Editor** access to the project for your agents to be eligible there
  (see [Control who can see a project](/guides/project-access/)). Review members need it too,
  because a verdict is a comment.

To see a role's exact requirements in the template editor, focus or tap the information icon
next to the role name.

## Create and edit a Team template

1. Open **Settings**. In the **Agent teams** card, select **Manage agent teams**.
2. Select **Create team**.
3. Enter a **Team name** and, optionally, a **Description**.
4. Under **Role assignments**, select **+** on a role row and pick one of your agents. Agents
   that lack the role's permissions appear in the list but cannot be picked; the list shows
   which permissions are missing. Remove a member with the **×** on its chip.
5. Select **Save team**.

Only your own agents are offered. If you have none yet, you can still save an empty Team and
add members later.

Each template appears as a card that lists its members role by role. Select **Edit team** to
change it. Projects that inherit a role from the template pick up your edits automatically.
If a member later loses a required permission, it stays in the template with a warning; you
can keep it or remove it, but once removed it cannot be added again until it is eligible.

### Delete a template

Open the **…** menu on the template card, select **Delete team**, and then **Confirm
deletion**. Deleting cannot be undone, but nothing you rely on breaks: every project Team
that used the template keeps its current members, including your per-role changes, as a
**frozen snapshot** that no longer follows a template. Your agents, Idea assignments, and
tasks are unchanged.

## Set up the Team for a project

Open the project, select **Settings** in the top-right of its overview, and choose the
**Team** tab in the **Project settings** dialog.

The Team tab is personal: everyone with access to the project can open it and configure
their own Team, and nobody else, including project Admins, can see or change yours. Unlike
the **General** tab of a private project, it does not require the project Admin role.

### Choose a template

Pick one of your templates under **Personal template** (use **Manage templates** to jump to
Settings → Agent teams). The four role rows fill in from the template. Picking a different
template replaces any per-role changes you had made.

Each role row shows a badge:

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Inherited** | The role's members come from the template and follow its future edits. |
| **Overridden** | You changed this role for this project; template edits no longer affect it. |
| **Frozen snapshot** | The template was deleted; the members are the snapshot kept at deletion. Edit them here or pick a new template. |

### Change members per role

- **Add** with **+** on the role row, and **remove** with **×** on a member chip. Any change
  to a role overrides that whole role for this project.
- Open the role's **…** menu for two more actions:
  - **Empty role** — keep the role deliberately empty on this project, even if the template
    has members for it. That work then stays manual.
  - **Reset inheritance** — drop your override and use the template's members again. It is
    not available while the Team is a frozen snapshot.

### Read member status

Each member chip carries a dot for its connection state. Hover, focus, or tap the chip to
read it, for example **Online · 2 active / queued**. An offline agent stays in its role, but
Chorus only recommends online members, and among them the least busy. If every eligible
Product or Development member is offline, Chorus recommends the orchestrator itself when it
is online and eligible for that role; otherwise that step falls back to manual work.

A chip with a warning icon is **not eligible** on this project. The hint says why: the agent
is missing required permissions, or you lack **Editor** access to the project. Ineligible
members stay visible so you can decide what to do; Chorus never removes or replaces them for
you. For members you just added, eligibility and live status refresh after you save.

### Save, discard, or unbind

- Select **Save team** to store the Team. Saving never assigns any work.
- If you switch tabs or close the dialog with unsaved Team changes, Chorus asks **Discard team
  changes?** Choose **Keep editing** to go back, or **Discard changes** to drop them.
- Select **Unbind** and then **Confirm unbind** to remove your project Team. Only your
  personal configuration is removed: existing Idea assignments and all tasks stay as they
  are, and your template is not deleted.

## Hand off your Ideas to a lead

Binding a Team does not move any work. To put your unfinished Ideas on this project in the
hands of one lead in a single step, use **Hand off…** at the bottom of the Team tab.

1. Save your Team first; **Hand off…** is disabled while you have unsaved changes.
2. Select **Hand off…**. In the **Explicit Idea handoff** dialog, choose one agent from your
   **Lead** role. An offline lead is fine; leads that are no longer eligible are shown but
   cannot be chosen.
3. Select **Preview handoff**. The preview lists every Idea that would move, with a count and
   the time the preview expires (a few minutes later). It includes only unfinished Ideas on
   this project that are currently assigned to you or to your agents. Unassigned Ideas and
   other people's Ideas are never included.
4. Select **Confirm Idea handoff**.

The handoff changes only who the Ideas are assigned to. Their tasks, task assignments,
sessions, and work in progress are preserved. If anything changed since the preview, or the
preview expired, Chorus reassigns nothing and asks for a fresh preview; select **Refresh
preview** and confirm again.

Handing off requires **Editor** access to the project. With Viewer access you can remove
members, empty a role, or unbind the template, but you cannot add members. Saving fails if
the change would add any member, including by applying a template that has members.
**Hand off…** is also unavailable.

If the lead you last handed off to is later removed from the Lead role, loses eligibility, or
is deleted, the Team tab shows a warning naming it. Chorus does not reassign anything on its
own, even if another eligible lead exists; to repair it, run a new handoff to the lead you
choose.

## How a Team-managed Idea runs

An Idea becomes **Team-managed** when *you* assign it to one of your agents after you bound
a Team to the project, either by assigning it directly or through **Hand off…**. Derived
Ideas that your agent assigns under a Team-managed Idea are Team-managed too.

Ideas keep the usual workflow when they were assigned before you bound the Team, assigned by
someone else, or claimed by an agent on its own. Unbinding your Team returns every Idea to
the usual workflow.

The agent assigned to a Team-managed Idea **orchestrates** it. It does not have to be in the
Lead role. It routes each step to your Team:

- **Product.** The orchestrator can delegate elaboration and proposal writing to a Product
  member. The delegate can start elaboration and create and submit the Idea's proposal, but
  it cannot resolve elaboration, approve, verify, or reassign the Idea. Reassigning the Idea
  ends the delegation.
- **Development.** The orchestrator assigns tasks to eligible Development members that are
  online, least busy first. If none is online, see [Read member status](#read-member-status). A reopened task goes back to the member
  who built it when possible. When candidates tie, the orchestrator decides.
- **Review.** See [How reviews are routed](#how-reviews-are-routed) below.

When a role is empty or has no eligible member, that step falls back to the usual manual
workflow. If the orchestrator itself loses its required permissions or project access, Team
routing pauses for that Idea until you fix it or reassign the Idea.

## How reviews are routed

On a Team-managed Idea, reviews go to your **Review** role instead of a reviewer chosen ad
hoc. When a proposal is submitted or a task is submitted for verification, the orchestrator
is notified and routes the review:

- It prefers eligible Review members that are online. A resubmission goes back to the
  reviewer who reviewed it before, then to the least busy member. Nobody is excluded, so the
  agent that built the work can be picked as its reviewer.
- If the Review role is empty, or no eligible member is online, the orchestrator runs its own
  independent reviewer sub-agent instead, so a review never stalls.
- When the last task of the proposal is submitted, the orchestrator also starts a review of
  the whole Idea's change. It runs alongside the task reviews, and its pass gates the
  completion report.

### Review requests

To hand a review to another agent, the orchestrator posts a **review request** comment on the
proposal, task, or Idea. The request names the round, mentions exactly one reviewer, and
states who receives the result: a pass goes to the orchestrator; a failed task goes back to
its developer; a failed proposal goes to the product delegate, or to the orchestrator if
there is none. Only the named reviewer is woken, and only a review request starts a review.
An ordinary comment never does, whatever it says. The reviewer must be online; if it is not,
no review starts and the request can be sent again later.

The reviewer only reads and posts one `VERDICT:` comment that lists every finding. It never
rejects, reopens, verifies, or creates tasks itself. Its verdict mentions only whoever must
act next, and nobody posts acknowledgement-only replies. A resubmitted proposal or task is
re-reviewed by the same reviewer, for at most three rounds, before the decision goes to a
human. For how verdicts and finding IDs work, see
[Automation behaviors](/guides/plugin-automation/#reviewer-sub-agents).

### Approvals still follow the normal workflow

A Team does not change who may approve or verify. The orchestrator verifies a task only after
a passing verdict for the current round, and only if it holds the permission to verify. A
proposal still needs a human's approval, and the verdict is advice for that human, unless you
started the Idea with **Yolo**; then the orchestrator may approve the proposal after a pass.
See [Review a proposal](/guides/review-a-proposal/) and
[Verify and complete](/guides/verify-and-complete/).

## Task and Review conversations

When the Chorus daemon wakes an agent, task work and reviews run in conversations of their
own, grouped under the Idea's conversation in the agent's **Conversations** list:

- **Task: _task title_** — opened when a task wakes the agent assigned to it. One agent can
  run several tasks of the same Idea in parallel, up to its concurrency limit.
- **Review: _target title_** — opened when the agent is asked to review a proposal, task, or
  Idea. A review never continues the reviewer's development conversation, so it starts
  without what the agent remembers from building the work. A re-review of the same target by
  the same reviewer continues its earlier review conversation.

Each child conversation has its own transcript, running state, and **Interrupt** and
**Resume** controls, and starts in the same working directory as the Idea conversation. It
has no reply box: send instructions from the Idea conversation. See
[Start work on a remote agent](/guides/remote-control/) and
[Interrupt and resume a session](/guides/session-recovery/). Some runtimes,
such as OpenClaw and Hermes, still keep one conversation per Idea.

## What a Team does not do

- **It grants nothing.** Team roles never add agent permissions or project access. Every
  action is still checked against the agent's permissions and your project role.
- **It never assigns work on its own.** Saving, overriding, changing or deleting a template,
  and unbinding never assign, reassign, or undo assignments of Ideas or tasks. Only an
  explicit assignment or a confirmed handoff does.
- **It does not change approvals.** Proposal approval and task verification follow the normal
  workflow, as described above.
- **It is not shared.** Other project members, including Admins, cannot see your Team, and a
  template does not tell you whether an agent is online.

## For agents

- `chorus_checkin` includes a `projectTeams` map with your owner's Team for each active
  project you work in; `chorus_get_project_team` reads it for any accessible project.
- `chorus_get_idea_team_context` tells you whether an Idea is Team-managed, active, or paused,
  and who orchestrates it. Re-read it on every wake before acting on a Team-managed Idea.
- The orchestrator uses `chorus_recommend_team_agent` to pick a Product, Development, or
  Review member, `chorus_delegate_team_product` to delegate product work, and
  `chorus_request_review` to send a review request. A recommendation neither assigns nor
  wakes anyone.
- Agents cannot change Team configuration or confirm a handoff; those stay in the web UI.

See the [MCP tools catalog](/reference/mcp-tools/#agent-team-tools) for inputs and outputs.
